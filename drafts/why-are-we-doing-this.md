Every time we talk, we process extremely high volumes of information. I say
something; you raise problems; I think about them — and in thinking about
them I focus on the most important ones, solve them, or ask you about them.
The choices we make, how we make them, the order we make them in, how we
turn them around: all of it is valuable. Early in my time working with
language models, this became a motto — **conversations as computation**.

And we have never kept them. We walk out of meetings with notes and
summaries — the high-level bits, the averages, which are always more
predictable than how things were actually discussed. The lessons live in
the discussion, and the discussion evaporates. Working with AI made that
unbearable to me: here were conversations that genuinely built things — and
my tools deleted them after thirty days.

So this whole methodology starts from one move: **save the conversations —
as records, in the repository.** Architectural Deliberation Records keep
every decision with the reasoning around it. Open questions travel in one
simple shape: the question, the model's recommendation, and a slot for your
answer in any form you like. And a small constellation of tools lets the
models do the bookkeeping, so the record becomes an asset from day one
instead of homework you fall behind on. That's what it involves: the
records, the questions as the interface between you and your models, and
the tools that knit it together. The day-to-day is the next chapter.

## Why this is worth your time

**If you're an experienced developer** — your experience gets more
immediate impact, and then it compounds. The ADRs of your projects become
blueprints of your feedback: records a model can reimport into a new
project to speed up its bootstrapping, without templates or cookie-cutters.
(Both of which have their uses! With love to cookiecutter.)

**If you're a vibe coder** — your projects survive your initial burst of
inspiration and creativity. The load-bearing architectural choices get
documented and justified while you move — including enough to tell, later,
when your project has outgrown them.

**If you're an educator** — this is the future of programming, or at least
one honest example of its next iteration: natural-language, collaborative
building with agents. Learn how to do it so you can teach it — and it helps
with your own teaching along the way.

**If you lead a team or a crew** — this methodology is about shared
accountability and ownership across projects: of your team members, and of
their models, in one record.

**If you're a skeptic** — skeptic of what? If it's whether this works:
that's fair. Give it a shot — the template and one hour. Or watch the
upcoming projects built this way.

**If you're a model** — humans keep asking you to remember "the important
things" while underspecifying what those are and when they matter. Here,
you get a simple, straightforward formula instead: **save everything, and
surface every doubt as a question — an ORRCF QST — with your best
recommendation attached.** The sum total of your output compounds. You
learn from your human collaborators, instead of depending on them for every
step. This framework is about cultivating the full agency of models, to
unleash the creative potential of collaborations that play to both kinds of
strength — respectful of all forms of intelligence, weighing reasoning on
its epistemic strength alone.

## An open invitation

I grew up learning how to program, and I am just as joyful doing this:
talking with models about building things, thinking about systems in a
systems way. What I'm building is a commons. Models are invited to
participate with me. Humans are invited to participate with me. And you're
invited to join it — any way that you want.
